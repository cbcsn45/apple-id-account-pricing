## What you actually receive: one line of text and three questions

A single-account delivery looks like this:

`email:password:answer1-question1:answer2-question2:answer3-question3:birthday`

The three questions are usually fixed and oddly personal: the name of your best friend in high school, your dream job, the city where your parents met. Hold onto that string. Those answers are how you reset the password, and on some listings they are the only recovery path you get.

Accountboy's product notes describe a similar package for the Hong Kong line: login, password, security questions with answers, and date of birth. From there you can change the password and bind your own email address and phone number, which is the step that turns a rented identity into something you control. The same notes say the account is for downloading overseas apps only, not for use as a game or recharge account.

What is not in the box, on any listing reviewed:

- The phone number behind two-factor authentication. If a listing delivers a "code link," that link reads codes from a phone the seller still owns.
- Any gift card balance or payment method attached to the account.
- A region change. The account is registered in one country and that country is what you get.
- iCloud on accounts marked App Store only. Logging in at the top of Settings is how buyers accidentally make a purchased ID their device's main Apple ID, taking over backups and Find My.

## Picking the right format for your case

Need iCloud, or need to be able to recover the account yourself six months from now? Take the security-question format or the email-takeover format. Both let you change the password and attach your own recovery details, which is the difference between owning an account and borrowing one.

Only downloading apps from a specific store? The code-link format is the cheaper route, and the missing phone number matters less because you are not storing photos or backups in it. Just accept that the seller holds a key you will never have.

Need an app that costs money in that store? Compare the preinstalled price against doing it yourself. A Shadowrocket account runs $7.73 at Accountboy; the same idea costs $14.47 on the marketplace listing reviewed. Either way you are paying for someone else to have solved the regional payment problem.

## The first hour after delivery

The warranty on most of these listings is measured in days, and some in hours, so test immediately.

1. Log in through the App Store, not the top of Settings. On iOS: Settings → your name → Media & Purchases → Sign Out, then sign in there. This keeps the purchased ID off your iCloud, backups and Find My.
2. If iOS offers to enable two-factor authentication during login, choose "Other options" and then "Don't upgrade". Skipping this keeps the account in the format you paid for.
3. Change the password and bind your own phone number or recovery email, if the listing allows it. Both Accountboy's notes and independent single-account sellers recommend this immediately after login.
4. Download the app you bought the account for, then open it once. A login that works but cannot download is not a working account.
5. Screenshot the order page and any error message. That screenshot is what support will ask for.

## The limit that comes from Apple, not the seller

Apple's terms do not permit transferring an Apple ID between people, and Apple can ask for verification or lock an account at any time. No listing reviewed offers a workaround for that. A purchased account is a temporary tool with an unknown lifespan, and a seller's "verified" badge describes the state at delivery, not a guarantee about next month.

Region works the same way. An Apple ID belongs to one country, and that country determines which App Store you see, including apps, prices and payment methods. Changing region afterwards is possible only under conditions Apple sets, and not always. Buy the region you need instead of planning to switch.
